How to use 7-Segment Logic Lab

The controller on the left has a limited number of outputs (bits). Build a circuit of logic gates that turns those bits into the 7 signals a–g that light the right digit on the display.

Suggested method

  1. Pick a task with 📋 Tasks, or choose your own settings in the top bar. A is always the most significant bit.
  2. In the Truth table, each column a–g shows when that segment pin must be 1 or 0.
  3. Choose one segment and open the Karnaugh map tab. Circle groups of 1s (you may include X's) and write a simplified expression.
  4. Build that expression with gates and wire the result to the segment's pin on the display.
  5. The Expressions tab shows the formula your wiring actually computes, so you can compare it with your own.
  6. When the column turns green ✓, move to the next segment. Challenge: use as few gates as possible!
  7. Stuck? The 💡 Solution tab reveals a suggested answer one segment at a time, shows its groups in the Karnaugh map, and can build the whole circuit for you.

Controls

  • Add: drag a component from the left panel, or click it. NOTE adds a text note (double-click it to edit).
  • Wire: drag from a pin (small circle) to another pin, or click one pin and then the other. The pins you can connect to light up blue. One output can feed many inputs.
  • Understand a gate: click it to see its truth table, its current values and the formula its output computes. Hover over a part to highlight its wires.
  • Move or remove a wire: drag it off the input pin. Drop it on empty space to delete it.
  • Select several: Shift+drag on the board or Shift+click. Ctrl+A selects everything.
  • Delete: Delete, or right-click a part. Copy/paste: Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V. Ctrl+D duplicates.
  • Inputs: click a row on the controller, press A B C D, click a truth-table row or K-map cell, or use ↑/↓.
  • Find mistakes: on the display, a dashed segment should be lit and a yellow segment should be off. ➜ Jump to a wrong digit takes you to the next digit that is still wrong.
  • More displays: choose 2–4 Displays in the top bar to show bigger numbers, e.g. 0–15 in decimal. The ones display is on the right. Above the tabs in the check panel you choose which display to check.
  • View: drag the empty board to pan; use the mouse wheel to zoom.
  • Undo/redo: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y. Your work is saved in this browser automatically. 🖼 Image saves a picture you can hand in.

Good to know

  • Unconnected gate inputs count as 0. Gates can have 2–4 inputs (select a gate and use +/−).
  • Common cathode: a segment lights when its pin is 1. Common anode: it lights when its pin is 0.
  • Don't care (X): input values the display never needs to show. Output whatever makes your circuit simplest.
